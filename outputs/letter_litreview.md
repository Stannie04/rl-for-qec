# Literature context: "Complementary decoders and a syndrome router for qLDPC codes"

*Ruijters & Krzywda letter draft (Overleaf `letter.tex`, 29 Oct 2026). Sources: 7 PDFs in `papers/` plus a light web search. No new downloads.*

**What the letter claims.** BP, BP+OSD and BP+LSD fail on nested sets of shots ($F_{\rm OSD}\subset F_{\rm BP}$). A SAC+NBP neural decoder fails on *different* shots: on the gross code at $p=5\times10^{-3}$, an oracle choosing between BP and NN reaches $8.2\times10^{-6}$, 11× below BP. A syndrome router that predicts "BP will fail" (NBP encoder + MLP, BCE loss) sends those shots to the NN and beats BP+OSD by 1.8–4.5×. The routing threshold gives a latency–accuracy trade-off. All results use code-capacity depolarizing noise.

**1. Hierarchical decoding and predecoding (the architecture is not new).** Delfosse's *lazy decoder* [2001.11427] defined the pattern: a cheap first stage handles easy syndromes and escalates hard ones. The cost model $\langle t\rangle=t_1+P_{\rm esc}\,t_2$ is the letter's latency equation. Pinball [2512.09807] (cryo-CMOS, surface code) and Arqade [2605.03180] extend this. Arqade automatically builds predecoders for *any qLDPC code, including [[144,12,12]]*, handles up to 99.98% of shots, and cuts OSD calls by 81% and Relay-BP legs by 60%. Zero-G [2608.02030] builds the strong decoder around this sparsity. The letter has to be positioned against Arqade on latency, because both reduce how often the expensive stage runs on the gross code.

**2. Confidence-gated composition (closest prior art).** Three papers already route shots between decoders:
- *Decoder Switching* (Toshio et al., Fujitsu, arXiv:2510.25222): a weak soft-output decoder hands off to a strong one. Accuracy ≥ the strong decoder at roughly the weak decoder's speed. Surface code.
- *Chongder* (arXiv:2607.05814): a confidence-gated NN that falls back to PyMatching. Surface code.
- **CoDecoder** [2610.28748, 20 Oct 2026]: a neural frontend with confidence routing to Relay-BP on BB codes ([[72,12,6]] plus one more), under *circuit-level* noise. It reports 22–50% fewer failures and 2.3–124× speed-ups. It also gives a per-shot analysis of *complementary errors* (only-NN, only-fallback and both-fail counts), which is the letter's heatmap argument. Its §VIII lists "train the network to complement its fallback" as future work, which is the letter's idea. It frames the method as *learning to defer* (Mozannar & Sontag).

The letter differs in direction (BP first, NN as the expert) and in one design choice: its router predicts BP failure only, so it can be trained without the second expert.

**3. BP post-processors.** BP+OSD (Panteleev–Kalachev; Roffe), BP+LSD, Relay-BP (2506.01779), AutDEC automorphism ensembles (2503.01738) and Chase-BP (2507.00254) all act on BP failures or decorrelate BP runs. The nesting $F_{\rm OSD}\subset F_{\rm BP}$ is close to true by construction, because OSD only runs where BP does not converge. A referee will treat it as expected, not as a finding. Relay-BP is the baseline a referee will ask for. A *mod-w defect-count rule* predicts BP convergence on BB codes with AUC 0.995 (2604.07995). It is a parameter-free router baseline that the letter has to beat.

**4. Neural and RL decoders for qLDPC.** NBP (Liu–Poulin 1811.07835), the Astra GNN (2408.07038, beats BP-OSD on BB codes), a transformer decoder for BB codes under circuit-level noise (2504.13043), QuBA (2510.06257), Cascade (2604.08358) and Logical-NBP (2608.27682) are all *more accurate on their own* than this letter's NN, which is worse than plain BP. Existing RL work on qLDPC decoding schedules BP (2603.10192) or targets BB codes with multi-agent RL (2601.19279). I found no SAC qubit-flip agent on an NBP encoder, so that combination looks new but is not a selling point on its own.

**Gaps a referee will press on.** (i) Code capacity only, while CoDecoder, Arqade and Decoder Switching all use circuit-level noise. (ii) No Relay-BP, AutDEC or Astra baseline. (iii) Latency compares CPU BP with a GPU NN, so the Pareto figure is not hardware-fair. (iv) Statistics: $2\times10^{-7}$ from $10^7$ shots is about 2 failures. (v) A router that matches the oracle may just be rediscovering the mod-w rule.

## Verdict

**Paper: yes, conditionally. Not PRL as currently scoped.** The complementarity heatmaps plus "BP-first router, interchangeable expert" make a clear, publishable message, but CoDecoder (9 days old) takes most of the novelty and has stronger experiments. To publish, the letter must: (a) cite CoDecoder, Decoder Switching and Arqade and state the difference (BP-first ordering, a router that does not depend on the expert, failure prediction on the Tanner graph); (b) add Relay-BP and the mod-w predictor as baselines; (c) add at least phenomenological or circuit-level noise on the gross code. Then target *PRR / Quantum / IEEE QCE*. With code capacity only, aim at a workshop or a thesis chapter.

**Patent: no.** Confidence-gated escalation from a fast decoder to a strong one is published (Delfosse 2020; Decoder Switching 2025, with Fujitsu as a likely applicant) and is close to claimed (US 11870462, NN classifier decoders). Using a learned failure predictor to select the expert is an obvious combination given CoDecoder and the learning-to-defer literature. A narrow hardware claim (BP on an FPGA with a shared, pre-trained NBP router) would be weak, and nothing points to commercial value. Arxiv the letter quickly instead: priority matters more than IP here.
